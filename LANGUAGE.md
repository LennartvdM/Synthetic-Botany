# Visual language: a notebook

Terms noticed in the reference pictures shared so far. This is a list of
observations, kept open on purpose. It ranks nothing, concludes nothing and
plans nothing. Add to it; do not tidy it into a system.

## From the flower photographs

- A wall whose angle changes more than once along its length: opens fast,
  runs parallel, opens again at the end.
- A rim that turns outward without breaking into folds.
- A mouth cut at a slant, one side longer than the other.
- Bodies overlapping like tiles, each pressed under the one above.
- Size and openness changing steadily along a row, closed at one end.
- Mouths packing into a surface.

## From the bulb pictures

- Thick plates wrapped over a core, standing off it, with crescent gaps.
- Slits that show a second body inside the first.
- A globe divided along its meridians.
- A body held in a crown of prongs with knobbed ends.
- A star of pointed tongues draped over the top of a globe.
- Small spheres gathered round a base.
- A tuft of fat tapering fingers from one point.
- Volume everywhere; nothing thin.

## From the concept sheet (six colour panels, eight sketches)

- One kind of elongated part, repeated and packed round an axis. The part
  changes between panels; the packing does not.
- Parts ending at different heights, so a column's surface is made of tips.
- The tip carrying the character of the part: open mouth with a pale rim,
  pale pad, wavy point, flat pointed scale, bead.
- A base zone where the same parts shorten and splay into a skirt.
- The same family of part shown twice in one picture: packed side by side
  in the large body, radiating from a point in the small one.
- One thin curling line joining a small form to a large one; thick against
  thin.
- A few thick trunks twisted round each other, straps peeling away.
- Hanging from above.
- Features on one side only: a row of pegs down one flank, a comb of teeth
  along an inner curve.
- A hood with an opening. A pair of long legs from a knot. A veil of fine
  fibres. A string of scales. A large pod in a nest of pointed bracts.

## How an asset is put together (given, not observed)

Stated with a worked example, a mussel-like form that bites. The example is
more specific than the scheme; any flora could fill the roles.

| role | in the example | what it is for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| body | the mussel | the form itself |
| base | meaty connective tissue | what it grows from; a rhizome |
| moving part | a tongue | any part that is animated or reacts; optional |
| flourish | barnacles | small things scattered at tasteful places: warts, tumours, fruiting bodies |
| rooting | roots | joins the base to the ground; kept separate from the base for flexibility |

Seen in the example sheet:

- The body is shown five times: five specimens of one species. (First read
  as one form in five states of opening. That was wrong.)
- Across the five, the build stays the same and the individual changes:
  how fat it is, how far the halves stand apart, how unequal the two halves
  are, which way it leans. A kind is recognised across specimens that
  differ.
- Two thick valves, streaked along their length, a different lining inside,
  a row of curved teeth down each edge, lumps where it seats.
- The base is a low mound with one or more seats, each a pad with raised
  concentric lips; it comes in sizes by number of seats.
- The flourish is a small cluster of a few plates, in a few variants, about
  a quarter of the body's size.
- The rooting is a sprawl of thin limbs from one knot, each ending in a
  flared foot, about as wide as the body is tall. It is the only thin part.

## Words used for the feeling

More fantastic. More volume. Not noisy. Substance. Deft.

Exaggerated. Beefy. Veiny. Comical. Exuberant. Gritty, in the manner of
French graphic novels. Not realistic.

## Recurring across more than one source

- Bundling: many parts sharing an axis (the cord of strands from the first
  renders; the packed columns).
- Parts that stand off each other so the gap between them reads.
- A different treatment at the tip or rim than along the length.

## Held rigid: the grit

Said after the first three asset kinds. The pout bell and the knuckle
bundle had it. The plated dome did not, and was called a regression: the
concept was fine, the style was broken. This is the one thing to be strict
about, so the work does not drift away from it.

What was said: it loses the grit.

My reading of the difference, to be corrected if wrong:

- Has it: parts that taper and swell; pads, lips and rolled edges; things
  that bulge where they meet; weight; cords standing proud of a surface;
  one family of part repeated with small differences.
- Breaks it: a perfect ball; flat round discs; crisp even gaps; anything
  that reads as a clean primitive placed on another clean primitive; a toy.

### Where the grit comes from (said of the bulb sketch)

The bodies are surrounded by a thick sheet. The bulges that flop out of it
have torn it half-way. So there is a fight between the torn fascia and the
thinner membrane of the bulge, which makes more expanse. That is a conflict
captured in a sculpt, and it is the outrageous grit, the double chin and
the pout that the earlier sketches were reaching for.

In short: something thick that binds, something softer that swells, and the
form shows the moment the first gave way to the second.

## Materials (given)

The work is fictional material design. "We don't have to build realistic
materials, we only have to build 'outrageous' materials that do only what
the artstyle needs them to do and then we can collapse the logic into a
distilate and apply it across all kinds of forms."

So a material here is a short list of behaviours, each turned up past what
a real stuff would do, and it is separate from the form it is put on.

First material tried (`tear.js`): a cover that clenches and rips, over a
bare skin that grows where stretched. Traits it has, as knobs: strength,
clench, thickness, expanse, tension, sag. What the pictures have that it
does not have yet, in the words already used on this page: a rolled lip on
the torn edge; weight, so that what comes out lies on what it came out of;
the cover itself doubling under the load.

Second material, wood (`wood.js`). One rule: every tip is the far end of a
strand that began as a root. Read off the pictures given for it: bundled,
winding, ribboned, crested, grooved, hollowed between strands, pointed at
every end, splayed at the ground. "Ancient." Leaves are not part of it.

### Wood, tense and relaxed (said of page 9)

Two moods of the same wood, both wanted.

- Relaxed: branches are straight runs joined by corners, and the wood
  keeps the shape it grew in. This is the wood for ordinary places.
- Tense: no corners, every strand pulled taut, a branch only bending
  slightly toward where it is going. Said of it: "very disturbing and
  icky", "tense and stressed". Kept on purpose: "high tautness in the
  early attempt still is usable for an evil biome. It genuinely feels
  uncomfortable to look at and that's good whenever the player should be
  disturbed."

To get the tense wood: `Trees.sprout({ kink: 0, set: 0 })`. Record of it:
`sketch/page9-tense-withdrawn.png` (withdrawn as the ordinary wood, not as
a look).
